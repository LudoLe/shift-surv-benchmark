\subsubsection{Real multi-center transfer}
We evaluate every directed transfer between the six I3LUNG centers, thirty in
all. Thirty transfers carry fewer than thirty independent observations: each
center is the target of five of them, so the same patients are scored
repeatedly. We therefore pool within target cohort and carry six disjoint units
into the random effects meta-analysis, which is the level at which the
independence assumption holds. The thirty unit analysis appears alongside it
and is uniformly less conservative; the six unit figures are the ones we quote.

Discrimination stays where ERM leaves it. Pooled gaps against ERM are $-0.013$
for IW-ERM ($p=0.003$), $-0.029$ for DANN ($p<0.001$), $-0.029$ for CDAN
($p<0.001$), and $-0.000$ for CORAL ($p=0.91$). The informative quantity is the
winner's curse bound: selecting for each target cohort whichever method
performed best on that cohort gives a pooled gap of $-0.000$ with a $95\%$
upper limit of $+0.005$. The pre-specified clinically relevant gain of $+0.020$
lies outside that interval, so the data support equivalence. The conclusion
comes from the width of the interval, and it therefore holds independently of
the power of any single comparison.

Calibration behaves differently, as it does in the synthetic benchmark. Platt
scaling improves it in both analyses, by $0.018$ across the six cohorts ($95\%$
CI $[0.008, 0.029]$, $p<0.001$) and by $0.015$ across the thirty pairs
($[0.005, 0.025]$, $p=0.004$). CDAN improves it by $0.014$ across cohorts
($[0.002, 0.026]$, $p=0.020$) and by $0.005$ across pairs, where the interval
covers zero ($p=0.32$); we therefore treat Platt scaling as the finding and
report CDAN as an effect that depends on the unit of analysis. DANN moves
calibration by $0.009$ with an interval that covers zero in both analyses. The
winner's curse bound reaches $+0.034$ across cohorts and $+0.028$ across pairs,
so a clinically relevant calibration gain remains possible under either. IW-ERM
worsens calibration by $0.016$ ($p<0.001$).

Prior recalibration is the informative case. Pooled it is the worst method by a
wide margin ($-0.071$, $p=0.001$), and with $I^2=90\%$ that pooled figure
describes no individual transfer. Its effect splits by target cohort: it is
close to neutral into SZMC ($+0.010$ on average, with two of the five source
centers improving by $+0.069$ and $+0.044$) and harmful into the other five,
where the average ranges from $-0.076$ to $-0.160$. This is the behaviour its
assumption predicts, since it corrects a shift in the class prior and leaves
every other difference in place. Identifying the cohorts where that assumption
holds requires target labels, which is the practical form of the paper's claim.

\footnote{Pooling raises rather than lowers the significance of IW-ERM on
discrimination ($p=0.089$ across thirty pairs, $p=0.003$ across six cohorts).
Averaging the five transfers that share a target removes variation between
source centers while leaving the systematic component, so the pooled estimate is
more precise despite resting on fewer units. The two analyses agree on every
conclusion we draw, with the single exception noted for CDAN.}
