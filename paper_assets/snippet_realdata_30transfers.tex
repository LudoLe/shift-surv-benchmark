\subsubsection{Real multi-center transfer}
We evaluate every directed transfer between the six I3LUNG centers, thirty in
all, rather than a chosen subset. Thirty transfers are not thirty independent
observations: each center appears as the target of five of them, so the same
patients are scored repeatedly. We therefore pool within target cohort and
carry six disjoint units into the random-effects meta-analysis, which is the
level at which the independence assumption holds. The thirty-unit analysis is
reported alongside it and is uniformly less conservative; we quote the six-unit
figures throughout.

Discrimination is not recovered by any method. Pooled gaps against ERM are
$-0.011$ for IW-ERM ($p=0.005$), $-0.033$ for DANN ($p<0.001$), $-0.028$ for
CDAN ($p<0.001$), and $-0.001$ for CORAL ($p=0.84$). The informative quantity
is the winner's-curse bound: selecting, for each target cohort, whichever
method performed best on that cohort, the pooled gap is $-0.000$ with a 95\%
upper limit of $+0.005$. A gain of the pre-specified clinically relevant size
$+0.020$ is therefore excluded by the data, not merely unobserved --- an
equivalence statement rather than an absence of evidence, and one that does not
rest on the power of any single comparison.

Calibration behaves differently, as it does in the synthetic benchmark. Platt
scaling improves ECE by $0.018$ ($p=0.003$), and the winner's-curse bound
reaches $+0.032$, so a clinically relevant calibration gain is \emph{not}
excluded. Prior recalibration is the informative case: pooled it is the worst
method by a wide margin ($-0.076$, $p=0.001$), but with $I^2=92\%$ the pooled
figure describes no individual transfer. It is the best method on transfers
into SZMC ($+0.069$ and $+0.041$ on the pairwise analysis) and harmful
elsewhere. That is the behaviour its assumption predicts: it corrects a shift
in the class prior and nothing else, so it helps exactly where the shift is
predominantly one of prevalence. Which centers those are is not knowable
without target labels, which is the practical form of the paper's claim.
