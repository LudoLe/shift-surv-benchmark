For calibration we fixed a directional rule before running the sweep: prior
adjustment was expected to improve ECE under isolated label-prior shift and to
worsen it under the other mechanisms. Configurations whose interval excluded
zero in the predicted direction were directionally consistent, configurations
whose interval contained zero were inconclusive, and configurations whose effect
was too small to resolve with the planned seeds were labelled uninformative. The
rule held: prior adjustment improved ECE in $88$ of $100$ configurations under
label-prior shift and worsened it in every other regime, in $66$ of $100$ under
covariate shift and $70$ of $100$ under measurement shift.

The two calibration effects that the benchmark reports for adversarial methods
were checked afterwards and against a different question, namely how often each
effect reappears as the generator configuration varies. We report the frequency
of replication rather than a verdict, since no direction was declared for them
in advance.

Those two checks required a change of protocol. The sweep holds hyperparameters
at one setting throughout, so that a change of outcome is attributable to the
generator configuration. DANN and CDAN are sensitive to that choice in a way the
other methods are not: held at one setting they raise target ECE, and selected
per run as the benchmark selects them they lower it. We therefore ran the two
adversarial methods with per-run selection inside the sweep and report both
results, since the reversal is itself informative. For every other method the
selection criterion moves target AUROC by at most $0.004$ across the grid, so
the frozen setting and the benchmark setting agree.
