## 1. Two verifications that change what the paper may claim

These were run against the actual generator, not reasoned about. Scripts are in the
scratchpad; the logic is reproducible from `generator/scenarios.py`.

### 1a. Baseline-hazard shift and label-prior shift ARE mathematically distinct

They are exact duals. Measured over 8 seeds, n=3000, RBF-MMD²:

| | baseline-hazard | label-prior |
|---|---|---|
| prevalence | 0.33 → 0.58 | 0.33 → 0.60 |
| MMD² of `P(x)` | **0.0000** (unchanged) | 0.0026 (moves) |
| MMD² of `P(x\|y=1)` | 0.0019 (moves) | **−0.0000** (invariant) |
| MMD² of `P(x\|y=0)` | 0.0031 (moves) | **−0.0001** (invariant) |

**Baseline-hazard shift leaves `P(x)` untouched and changes `P(y|x)`.
Label-prior shift leaves `P(x|y)` untouched and changes `P(y)`, hence moves `P(x)`.**

So label-prior shift *is* exactly the label-shift setting, and baseline-hazard shift is
*not*, even though both raise event prevalence by a similar amount. This is precisely why
Saerens prior adjustment recovers calibration in one and harms it in the other — the
assumption holds exactly in one case and is violated in the other. The distinction is
real and worth stating explicitly in the Benchmark section.

### 1b. The oracle is a recoverability reference, NOT a target ceiling under concept shift

Source-fitted oracle vs an oracle refitted on target labels, both on the true `u_inv`,
10 seeds, n=2000:

| regime | oracle (source-fit) | oracle (target-fit) | ERM |
|---|---|---|---|
| covariate | 0.827 | 0.828 | 0.736 |
| measurement | 0.827 | 0.828 | 0.630 |
| baseline hazard | 0.849 | 0.850 | 0.795 |
| label-prior | 0.820 | 0.821 | 0.770 |
| **concept** | **0.604** | **0.820** | 0.591 |

In five of six regimes the two coincide to within 0.001, so the source-fitted oracle is a
valid reference there. **Under concept shift it is not**: the latent factors still carry
0.82 AUROC at the target, but a source-trained model cannot reach it.

**The paper's current sentence is therefore wrong.** It says the oracle's fall to 0.61 is
"an internal check that the benchmark registers little recoverable signal when the outcome
mechanism changes". There *is* signal — 0.82 of it. What is missing is transportability.
The corrected reading is stronger for the paper's thesis: the limit is identifiability,
not information.

Naming: "latent-oracle reference" / "recoverability reference" is accurate; "ceiling" and
"upper bound" are not. Applies to figure captions too ("invariant-oracle ceiling").

## 2. Two smaller checks

**The `−0.03` ECE is not a sign error.** `i3lung_meta.csv` has
`ece, method:prior_recalib, −0.03154`, and the figure axis is "ECE gain (ERM − method)".
Under that convention a negative gain does mean prior adjustment made calibration worse,
so "backfires" is right. But the prose says "pooled ECE change −0.03" without naming the
convention, which is why it read as a sign error. Fix the wording, not the number:
"increases pooled ECE by 0.03".

**The "one mechanism at a time" claim is only true for three of six regimes.** From the
code: `baseline_hazard_shift` (λ₀ only), `prior_shift` (resampling only) and
`concept_shift` (w only) are clean. But `covariate_shift` also raises σ from 0.5 to 0.8,
`observational_shift` moves μ_inv, μ_spu *and* σ *and* rotates A_inv, and
`spurious_shift` moves μ_spu as well as the cross-covariance. Any table that lists only
one changed parameter for those three is inaccurate — the honest fix is to list them, not
to compress the column.
